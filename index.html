02 — Current thesis

Ongoing · Sep 2026 –

Evaluating a Vision Language Model Clinical Decision Support Pipeline on Handwritten Outpatient Prescriptions in a Bangladeshi Private Hospital

Undergraduate thesis · Metropolitan University, Sylhet · Manuscript in preparation (Moosa SM, Emon MH, Muzammil M)

The problem

Most outpatient prescriptions in Bangladesh are handwritten and dispensed with no automated safety check. Clinical decision support depends on structured digital data, and traditional OCR fails on cursive handwriting, abbreviations and mixed Bangla–English notation. As a result, safety checks never receive usable input.

The pipeline

  1. 01Image captureDe-identified prescription scans, annotated by clinicians as ground truth
  2. 02VLM extractionDrug, strength, dose, frequency and duration as structured data
  3. 03NormalizationBrand names mapped to generic ingredients via a Bangladesh drug database
  4. 04CDS screeningRule-based checks for interactions, dose limits and duplication

Research questions

  1. How accurately can vision language models extract drug name, dose, route and frequency from handwritten Bangladeshi prescriptions?
  2. How do extraction errors propagate into downstream CDS alerts, as missed and false hazards?
  3. What share of real outpatient prescriptions contain preventable hazards that a VLM-CDS pipeline could flag?

Evaluation

Field-level extraction precision, recall and F1 against clinician annotations, plus hazard-detection sensitivity and false-alert rate against clinician review.

Expected contributions

  • Empirical The first local estimate of preventable prescribing hazards in handwritten outpatient prescriptions at this setting.
  • Technical A benchmark of VLMs on low-resource, mixed-script medical handwriting.
  • Safety Measures how extraction errors affect CDS reliability, to inform human-in-the-loop design.
  • Policy Evidence for medication-safety investment where digital prescribing is still rare.
From research to product

Duha Rx runs real-time CDS on digital prescriptions. The VLM pipeline extends those checks to the paper prescriptions still in circulation at pharmacies and on intake. Next steps: integrate validated CDS rules into Duha Rx, add Bangla-language support, and release a public screening tool with clear accuracy disclaimers.
